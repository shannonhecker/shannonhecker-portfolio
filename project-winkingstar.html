Winking Star

I designed Winking Star so either parent could see what was left at bedtime and each child could mark a routine as done.

about.

Overview

When one parent took over at bedtime, we had to ask which routines were done. Our children had stopped looking at their chore charts.

My role

I designed the app for my family outside work. My two children were its first users, and I watched what they did just after finishing a routine.

Design process

I tried large icons, stickers and small celebrations. One tap now earns a star. Over the week, the stars grow a Mystery Pet. A missed evening leaves the stars already earned in place.

Recognising their own board

A child can choose a dinosaur or an ocean scene for their board. I used the same artwork on iPhone and web so they can recognise their space before reading their name.

One family across screens

On the web, I kept the week together. On iPhone, Home, Activity, Treasure and Progress have separate tabs, so a child can mark an activity without navigating a full week. The same family data carries across devices. A second adult joins the existing board and sees where the first left off.

Visit winkingstar.com ↗ (opens in new tab)

Handing a child the phone

Kid Mode lets a child tick off reading on their own. Purchases and changes to the family board sit behind a parental check.

Outcomes

Winking Star is live on the web and iOS in five languages. Since launch I have fixed the midnight rollover, improved VoiceOver support and refined weekly stars and treasures. My next check is to watch a second adult join a family board without my help.
